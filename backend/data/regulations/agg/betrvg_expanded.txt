Betriebsverfassungsgesetz (BetrVG)
Betriebsverfassungsgesetz vom 15. Januar 1972 (BGBl. I S. 13)
Source: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/

---

agg BetrVG §1
Title: Works council formation — mandatory in establishments with 5 or more eligible employees
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Establishments (Betriebe) with at least 5 permanent employees eligible to vote, of whom 3 are eligible to stand for election
Text: Works councils (Betriebsraete) must be elected in all establishments with at least five permanent employees who are eligible to vote, provided at least three of them are eligible to stand for election. The obligation to hold works council elections rests with the employer. Works councils represent all employees in the establishment, including part-time and fixed-term employees. In groups of companies, a group works council (Konzernbetriebsrat) may be established. The establishment threshold applies to the individual establishment (Betrieb), not the company as a whole. Employers may not prevent or obstruct works council elections; doing so is a criminal offence under §119 BetrVG. Once a works council exists, extensive co-determination rights apply under §§87 et seq.
Keywords: BetrVG, Betriebsrat, works council, 5 employees, election threshold, establishment, employer obligation, Betrieb

agg BetrVG §87
Title: Works council co-determination — technical monitoring systems, working time, and performance monitoring
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All employers with an established works council (i.e. 5+ eligible employees in the establishment)
Text: The works council has mandatory co-determination rights in social matters including: the arrangement of daily work start and end times including breaks; temporary extension or reduction of normal working hours; time, place, and method of wage payment; setting up and using technical devices intended to monitor employee conduct or performance (technische Einrichtungen die dazu bestimmt sind das Verhalten oder die Leistung der Arbeitnehmer zu ueberwachen); principles of remuneration and introduction of new remuneration methods; setting performance-related pay rates; general holiday schedules; introduction of short-time work; safety regulations; and management of company housing. Any implementation of employee monitoring technology — including GPS tracking, productivity monitoring software, email scanning, CCTV, and AI-based performance assessment — requires prior works council agreement. Without a works council agreement, the employer may not implement such systems. Disputes are resolved by a binding conciliation board (Einigungsstelle).
Keywords: Mitbestimmung, co-determination, technical monitoring, §87 BetrVG, performance monitoring, working time, CCTV, employee surveillance, conciliation board

agg BetrVG §99
Title: Works council approval required before hiring, grading, transfer, or reassignment
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Employers with more than 20 employees eligible to vote who have a works council
Text: In establishments with more than 20 employees eligible to vote, the employer must inform the works council before every hiring, grading, regrading, or transfer and obtain the works council's approval. The employer must provide all relevant documents including application materials and personal information. The works council has one week to grant or withhold approval in writing. Silence constitutes approval. The works council may refuse approval if the hiring or transfer would violate laws, regulations, or collective agreements; would cause disproportionate disadvantage to other employees; the required internal job posting was omitted; or there is concern that workplace peace would be disturbed through unlawful conduct. If the works council refuses, the employer must apply to the labour court. Hiring a person without proper works council involvement renders the employment relationship not automatically void but the employer is subject to enforcement.
Keywords: Einstellung, hiring approval, Versetzung, transfer, works council consent, one week, labour court, §99 BetrVG, 20 employees

agg BetrVG §102
Title: Works council consultation mandatory before every dismissal — dismissal without hearing is invalid
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All employers with an established works council — applies to every dismissal regardless of company size
Text: The employer must consult the works council before every dismissal and must disclose to the works council the reasons for the dismissal. A dismissal effected without prior consultation of the works council is void (unwirksam). The works council must be given one week for ordinary dismissals and three days for extraordinary dismissals to express its position. If the works council objects in writing within these periods, the employer must continue employing the employee until the final resolution of any unfair dismissal claim. The works council may object on grounds including: neglect of social considerations in selection; availability of alternative employment in the company; possibility of retraining; or that the employee would accept modified contract terms. A failure to properly consult the works council — even if the dismissal is otherwise justified — renders the dismissal legally void.
Keywords: Betriebsratsanhoerung, works council consultation before dismissal, §102 BetrVG, dismissal void, one week, continued employment, Kuendigungsschutz

agg BetrVG §111
Title: Works council rights in major operational changes — employer must inform and consult before any Betriebsaenderung
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Companies with more than 20 employees eligible to vote; in companies with more than 300 employees the works council may engage an external advisor
Text: In companies with more than 20 employees eligible to vote, the employer must inform the works council in a timely and comprehensive manner about planned major operational changes (Betriebsaenderungen) that would cause significant disadvantages for employees or substantial parts of the workforce, and must consult with the works council about the planned changes. The following are defined as operational changes requiring notification and consultation: (1) reduction or closure of the entire operation or major divisions; (2) relocation of the company or significant operational units; (3) merger with other operations or division of existing operations; (4) fundamental restructuring of organisational procedures, business purpose, or facilities; and (5) introduction of substantially new work methods or manufacturing processes. The employer must attempt to reach a reconciliation of interests (Interessenausgleich) with the works council. If the employer implements an operational change without attempting a reconciliation of interests, affected employees may claim a nachteilsausgleich (disadvantage compensation). A social plan (Sozialplan) is mandatory for operational changes causing economic disadvantages to employees. In companies with more than 300 employees, the works council may engage an external advisor at the employer's expense.
Keywords: Betriebsaenderung, operational change, reconciliation of interests, Interessenausgleich, Sozialplan, nachteilsausgleich, closure, relocation, 20 employees, 300 employees, external advisor, §111 BetrVG
