gwg §2  (bafin_gwg_obligated_entities)
Title: GwG obligated entities — who is subject to AML/CFT obligations under §2 GwG
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All entities listed in §2 GwG regardless of size
Text: The Geldwäschegesetz (GwG) applies to a specific list of obligated entities (Verpflichtete) enumerated in §2 GwG. The main categories are: credit institutions and financial services institutions (banks, payment service providers, e-money institutions — supervised by BaFin); insurance undertakings offering life insurance or other investment-related products; investment firms and management companies; financial agents; lawyers (Rechtsanwälte), notaries (Notare), and other independent legal professionals when they assist clients with certain transactions (real estate, company management, account management, trust/company formation); auditors (Wirtschaftsprüfer), tax advisers (Steuerberater), and certified accountants; real estate agents (Immobilienmakler) — for purchase and rental transactions with annual rent ≥€10,000; gambling operators (casinos, online gambling); dealers in high-value goods (Güterhändler) where cash payments ≥€10,000 are accepted; art dealers and auction houses for cash transactions ≥€10,000; trust and company service providers; crypto asset service providers (VASPs). Each category of obligated entity has a sector-specific supervisory authority: BaFin (financial institutions), Bundesnotarkammer (notaries), Rechtsanwaltskammern (lawyers), Wirtschaftsprüferkammer (auditors), Steuerberaterkammern (tax advisers), Gewerbeaufsichtsämter (real estate agents, high-value goods dealers), gambling regulators. Obligated entities that are not in scope can still be affected if they accept payments on behalf of entities that are.
Keywords: GwG §2, obligated entities, Verpflichtete, AML scope, BaFin, real estate, lawyers, notaries, auditors, crypto, casinos, high-value goods, financial services, sector supervisors

gwg §5  (bafin_gwg_risk_analysis_sec5)
Title: §5 GwG — AML/CFT risk analysis obligation for all obligated entities
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All obligated entities under §2 GwG regardless of size
Text: Section 5 GwG requires every obligated entity to conduct and document a comprehensive analysis of the money laundering and terrorist financing risks relevant to its specific business. The risk analysis must consider: the products, services, and transactions offered by the entity; the customer base (types, geographic origin, industries, legal forms); the distribution and delivery channels used to provide services; and the geographic areas in which the entity operates. The risk analysis must identify, categorise (low/standard/high), and assess all significant AML/CFT risks. It must be documented in writing, approved by senior management, and made available to the competent supervisory authority on request. The risk analysis must be updated when there are material changes to the business model, customer base, product range, geographic exposure, or applicable regulatory requirements, and must be reviewed at least annually even without changes. BaFin's Auslegungs- und Anwendungshinweise (AuA) for credit institutions specify that the risk analysis must cover: institutional risk from customers, products, geographic exposure, and delivery channels; documentation must be sufficiently granular for supervisors to assess the quality of the analysis; generic or template-based analyses without entity-specific content do not satisfy the legal requirement. The risk analysis must be the foundation for all AML/CFT measures — customer due diligence intensity, monitoring thresholds, and internal controls must be calibrated to the risks identified.
Keywords: GwG §5, risk analysis, money laundering risk, terrorist financing, Risikoanalyse, obligated entity, BaFin AuA, annual review, risk-based approach, customer risk, geographic risk, product risk

gwg §10  (bafin_gwg_standard_cdd_sec10)
Title: §10 GwG — standard customer due diligence (CDD) requirements
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All obligated entities — for all business relationships and certain transactions
Text: Standard customer due diligence (CDD) under §10 GwG consists of four core measures that must be applied when establishing a business relationship, for occasional transactions ≥€15,000 (or ≥€10,000 in cash), when there are grounds to suspect money laundering or terrorist financing, or when there are doubts about previously obtained identification data. The four measures are: (1) Identification of the customer — natural persons: name, date of birth, nationality, address verified by official identity document (passport, national ID card); legal entities: firm name, legal form, registered office, management personnel, registry number; identification must be based on documents from a reliable and independent source. (2) Identification of the beneficial owner (wirtschaftlich Berechtigter) — see §3 GwG and §13 GwG for requirements. (3) Understanding the purpose and intended nature of the business relationship — what products/services will be used, for what business purpose, what is the expected transaction volume; this must be documented. (4) Continuous monitoring of the business relationship — monitoring transactions against the customer's risk profile; updating CDD data when material changes occur; enhanced scrutiny of complex or unusual transactions. CDD must be completed before the business relationship is established; if this is not possible for legitimate operational reasons, it must be completed as soon as practicable without delay. Where CDD cannot be completed, the business relationship must not be established or must be terminated, and a suspicious activity report (SAR) under §43 GwG must be considered.
Keywords: §10 GwG, standard CDD, customer identification, KYC, beneficial owner, purpose of business relationship, ongoing monitoring, business relationship, transaction monitoring, identity verification

gwg §3  (bafin_gwg_beneficial_owner_sec3_sec19)
Title: §3 and §19 GwG — beneficial owner identification and Transparenzregister
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All obligated entities when establishing a business relationship with a legal entity
Text: Identifying the beneficial owner (wirtschaftlich Berechtigter) is one of the most important and complex CDD obligations. Under §3 GwG, the beneficial owner of a legal entity is the natural person who ultimately owns or controls the entity — defined as a natural person who directly or indirectly holds more than 25% of the capital shares or voting rights, or who exercises control in another manner. Where no natural person meets the 25% threshold, the senior management personnel (Geschäftsführer, Vorstand) are recorded as the beneficial owner by default (fictive beneficial owner). For multi-layered structures (holding companies, trusts), obligated entities must look through all layers to identify the ultimate natural person beneficiary. The Transparenzregister (Transparency Register) maintained by the Bundesanzeiger Verlag contains beneficial ownership declarations from legal entities. Since October 2022, the Transparenzregister operates as a full register — all legal entities must actively register their beneficial owner data (previously many entities could rely on disclosure in the commercial register). Obligated entities must: consult the Transparenzregister before establishing a business relationship; not rely solely on register data — they must independently verify and assess the plausibility of register information using risk-based judgment; document any discrepancies between register data and information obtained from the customer; report discrepancies to the register authority. Where beneficial ownership is deliberately obscured through complex structures, this is itself a high-risk factor triggering enhanced due diligence.
Keywords: beneficial owner, wirtschaftlich Berechtigter, §3 GwG, §19 GwG, Transparenzregister, 25% threshold, look-through, fictive beneficial owner, legal entities, KYC, transparency register, discrepancy reporting

gwg §13  (bafin_gwg_pep_requirements)
Title: §13 GwG — enhanced due diligence for politically exposed persons (PEPs)
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All obligated entities when the customer or beneficial owner is a PEP or PEP associate
Text: Politically exposed persons (PEPs) require mandatory enhanced customer due diligence (ECDD) under §13 GwG. A PEP is a natural person who currently holds or has held a prominent public function within the past 12 months (1 year), including: heads of state or government, ministers and deputy ministers, members of national parliaments, members of supreme courts, members of courts of auditors or boards of central banks, ambassadors and senior military officers (general officer grade and above), members of administrative and supervisory bodies of state-owned enterprises, directors of international organisations. PEPs also include their immediate family members (spouses, civil partners, children and their spouses, parents) and known close associates (persons with joint business ownership or other close business relations). Obligated entities must maintain and consult a PEP screening list as part of their CDD process — using commercial databases is standard practice. When a PEP relationship is identified, the following enhanced measures are mandatory: senior management approval before establishing or continuing the business relationship; adequate measures to establish the source of wealth and the source of funds of the PEP; enhanced ongoing monitoring of the business relationship. The PEP status continues for a minimum of 12 months after the person leaves their prominent public function — risk-based assessment determines if ECDD continues thereafter. Former PEPs can be returned to standard CDD if the risk assessment supports this. Domestic PEPs (German politicians, officials) are subject to the same requirements as foreign PEPs under the current GwG.
Keywords: PEP, politically exposed person, §13 GwG, enhanced due diligence, source of funds, source of wealth, senior management approval, PEP screening, family members, close associates, 12 months

gwg §15  (bafin_gwg_enhanced_cdd_sec15)
Title: §15 GwG — enhanced due diligence triggers and measures for high-risk situations
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All obligated entities — when high-risk factors are identified
Text: Section 15 GwG requires enhanced customer due diligence (ECDD) in all cases where a higher risk of money laundering or terrorist financing is identified. Mandatory ECDD triggers include: the customer is from or the transaction involves a high-risk third country on the EU list of jurisdictions with strategic AML/CFT deficiencies (currently maintained by the EU Commission under AMLA framework); the business relationship or transaction is particularly complex or opaque; the transaction involves unusually large amounts without apparent legitimate purpose; the customer's ownership or control structure is particularly complex or opaque without credible economic explanation; the customer is a correspondent banking relationship; a PEP is involved (see §13 GwG). Enhanced measures that may be applied include (must be proportionate to the risk): obtaining additional information about the customer and the beneficial owner; obtaining additional information about the intended nature of the business relationship; obtaining information about the source of funds and the source of wealth of the customer and the beneficial owner; obtaining approval from senior management before establishing or continuing the business relationship; conducting enhanced ongoing monitoring of the business relationship, increasing the frequency and intensity of transaction monitoring. BaFin guidance emphasises that ECDD is not a fixed menu of measures — obligated entities must apply measures that are genuinely proportionate to the specific risks identified, documented in the customer file with reasoning.
Keywords: §15 GwG, enhanced CDD, high-risk third countries, ECDD triggers, source of funds, senior management approval, complex structures, EU high-risk list, correspondent banking, PEP enhanced measures

gwg §14  (bafin_gwg_simplified_cdd_sec14)
Title: §14 GwG — simplified due diligence for lower-risk customers
Obligation: MAY (at entity's discretion when criteria are met)
Threshold: All obligated entities — applicable only to customers classified as low-risk
Text: Section 14 GwG permits obligated entities to apply simplified customer due diligence (SCDD) where the risk of money laundering and terrorist financing has been assessed as lower, based on specific criteria. Simplified CDD does not mean no CDD — it means the intensity and depth of measures can be reduced proportionately to the lower risk. Factors that may justify simplified CDD include: the customer is a domestic credit institution or financial services institution subject to equivalent AML/CFT regulation; the customer is a publicly listed company on a regulated market in the EU or in a jurisdiction with equivalent disclosure requirements; the customer is a public authority; the product or service carries structurally low ML/TF risk (e.g. basic current accounts for domestic individuals, small premium life insurance policies, pension funds with no surrender value). Even where SCDD applies: the customer must still be identified; the business relationship must still be monitored, albeit at a lower intensity; simplified CDD must be abandoned and standard or enhanced CDD applied immediately if risk indicators emerge. SCDD cannot be applied to customers where specific high-risk factors are present regardless of the general risk classification. BaFin supervisors scrutinise SCDD decisions closely — the basis for classifying a customer as lower risk must be documented in the customer file with sufficient specificity. Applying SCDD without documented justification is a compliance violation.
Keywords: §14 GwG, simplified CDD, SCDD, low-risk customers, regulated institution, public authority, listed company, proportionate measures, risk justification, customer file documentation

gwg §43  (bafin_gwg_sar_reporting_sec43)
Title: §43 GwG — suspicious activity reporting (SAR) to the German FIU
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All obligated entities — upon formation of suspicion regardless of transaction amount
Text: Section 43 GwG imposes a mandatory obligation to submit a suspicious activity report (Verdachtsmeldung) to the German Financial Intelligence Unit (Zentralstelle für Finanztransaktionsuntersuchungen — FIU) at the Zollkriminalamt. The obligation arises when the obligated entity, its staff, or its management become aware of facts that indicate that assets subject to a business relationship or transaction could be related to money laundering or terrorist financing. There is no minimum transaction threshold for SAR reporting. The SAR must be submitted before the transaction is carried out where possible; where this is not possible, the SAR must be submitted immediately after the suspicious facts become known, and the suspicious transaction may be completed if not submitting would make it more difficult to apprehend the suspected criminal. The FIU can be reached via their online reporting portal (goAML Web). The SAR must contain: description of the suspicious facts; customer identification data; relevant transaction data; the basis for suspicion. Tipping-off prohibition (§47 GwG): the customer and third parties must not be informed about the SAR submission or the FIU investigation — this prohibition is absolute and violation is a criminal offence. Legal protection: obligated entities and their employees who submit SARs in good faith are exempt from civil liability for breach of confidentiality obligations. Management must be immediately informed of all SAR submissions. SAR statistics and quality are reviewed by BaFin during inspections — failure to submit SARs in cases where suspicion was or should have been formed is a serious compliance failure.
Keywords: §43 GwG, SAR, suspicious activity report, Verdachtsmeldung, FIU, Zollkriminalamt, goAML, tipping-off prohibition, §47 GwG, legal protection, money laundering suspicion, terrorist financing

gwg §8  (bafin_gwg_record_keeping_sec8)
Title: §8 GwG — record keeping obligations for CDD documentation
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All obligated entities — for all CDD records and transaction documentation
Text: Section 8 GwG requires obligated entities to retain all documents and records relating to their AML/CFT obligations. Retention periods: CDD identification documents (copies of identity documents, beneficial ownership records, risk assessment documentation) must be retained for 5 years after the end of the business relationship; transaction records must be retained for 5 years after completion of the transaction; SAR submissions and related documentation must be retained for 5 years. In cases where the supervisory authority orders extended retention, records must be kept for up to 10 years. Records must be kept in a manner that allows them to be made available to competent authorities (supervisory authority, law enforcement) without undue delay — typically within 2 business days on request. Electronic record-keeping is fully acceptable, including scanned copies of physical documents, provided that the integrity and completeness of the records can be verified. Records must be protected against destruction, loss, or unauthorised access. For customer files, all information collected during CDD including the basis for risk assessments and ECDD/SCDD decisions must be documented with sufficient detail to allow reconstruction of the decision-making process. BAFIN guidance emphasises that thin or generic documentation (e.g. "standard risk customer, CDD completed") without specifics does not meet the documentation standard. Inspections by BaFin routinely include sample review of customer files for CDD documentation completeness.
Keywords: §8 GwG, record keeping, 5-year retention, CDD records, transaction records, SAR retention, customer file, electronic records, BaFin inspection, documentation standard, 10-year extension

gwg §6  (bafin_gwg_internal_safeguards_sec6)
Title: §6 GwG — internal safeguards: employee training, reliability screening, whistleblowing channel
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All obligated entities — specific organisational requirements
Text: Section 6 GwG requires obligated entities to implement internal AML/CFT safeguards appropriate to the nature and size of the business, alongside the separate obligation under §7 GwG to appoint a money laundering reporting officer. The §6 requirements are: (1) Employee training: all employees involved in or relevant to AML/CFT compliance must receive training on the legal requirements, the entity's internal AML procedures, current money laundering typologies, and the SAR reporting procedure; training must be conducted at minimum annually and documented; records must show individual employee name, date, content, and completion. (2) Reliability screening of employees: employees must be assessed for reliability (Zuverlässigkeit) at hiring in AML-relevant roles; ongoing monitoring for changes that affect reliability. (3) Whistleblowing channel: obligated entities must maintain a channel through which employees can confidentially report AML violations; this channel must be independent from the line management of the AML officer.
Keywords: §6 GwG, internal safeguards, employee training, reliability screening, whistleblowing, AML internal controls, organisational requirements

gwg §7  (bafin_gwg_mlro_appointment_sec7)
Title: §7 GwG — appointment of a Geldwäschebeauftragter (money laundering reporting officer, MLRO) and deputy
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Obligated entities under §2(1) Nr. 1-3, 6, 7, 9 and 15 GwG (credit and financial-services institutions, payment and e-money institutions, insurance and gambling undertakings among others); supervisory authority may order appointment for other categories, and may exempt smaller entities on request
Text: Section 7 GwG requires obligated entities listed in §2(1) Nr. 1 to 3, 6, 7, 9 and 15 GwG to appoint a money laundering reporting officer (Geldwäschebeauftragter, MLRO) at management level, together with a deputy (§7(1)). The MLRO is responsible for compliance with AML/CFT law, without relieving senior management of its own responsibility, and reports directly to management. The competent supervisory authority may exempt an obligated entity from the duty to appoint an MLRO where it is satisfied that the risk of information loss from a divided organisational structure does not exist and that other risk-based safeguards are in place (§7(2)). The supervisory authority may also order entities under §2(1) Nr. 4, 5, 8, 10 to 14 and 16 GwG to appoint an MLRO where it considers this appropriate, and as a rule will do so for dealers in high-value goods whose main activity is trading in such goods (§7(3)). The appointment of the MLRO and deputy, and any subsequent removal, must be notified to the supervisory authority in advance; the authority may require an MLRO to be replaced if they lack the necessary qualification or reliability (§7(4)). The MLRO must perform the role domestically, act as the point of contact for law enforcement, the Financial Intelligence Unit, and the supervisory authority, and must be given sufficient authority, resources, and unhindered access to all relevant information, data, records, and systems, reporting directly to senior management (§7(5)). Data obtained by the MLRO may be used only to fulfil their duties (§7(6)). The MLRO and deputy are protected against dismissal or other employment disadvantage on account of performing their duties, including for one year after leaving the role, except for good cause (§7(7)).
Keywords: §7 GwG, Geldwäschebeauftragter, money laundering reporting officer, MLRO appointment, deputy, exemption, notification to supervisory authority, employment protection, senior management reporting

gwg §2  (bafin_gwg_real_estate_specifics)
Title: GwG obligations for real estate agents — §2(1) Nr. 14 GwG
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Real estate agents (Immobilienmakler) — for covered transactions
Text: Real estate agents (Immobilienmakler) are obligated entities under §2(1) Nr. 14 GwG and must apply full AML/CFT obligations when they mediate: purchase and sale of real estate (regardless of value); rental of real estate where the annual rent exceeds €10,000. Real estate agents are supervised by the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt (trade supervision authority) of the relevant federal state, not by BaFin. The obligations apply from the moment the agent begins to provide services to a client, not only when a transaction is completed. Specific requirements for real estate agents: identification of both buyer/tenant and seller/landlord where the agent represents both parties; beneficial owner identification for legal entity clients; verification that the purchase price or rent is consistent with market rates (significant undervaluation may indicate money laundering); particular attention to cash payments and unusual payment structures (payment by third parties, split payments just below threshold); enhanced scrutiny of transactions involving offshore or opaque ownership structures in the property title chain. Common ML typologies in real estate that agents must be alert to: purchase of high-value property with no apparent connection to disclosed income; rapid resale at inflated prices to realise cash; use of shell companies to obscure beneficial ownership of property; simultaneous purchase and sale transactions involving related parties at different prices. Real estate agents must register with the Transparenzregister as obligated entities and establish a documented risk analysis under §5 GwG specific to their real estate business.
Keywords: real estate agents, Immobilienmakler, §2(1) Nr. 14, annual rent €10,000, property transaction, Gewerbeaufsichtsamt, Transparenzregister, cash payment, beneficial owner, ML typologies, offshore structures

gwg §2  (bafin_gwg_crypto_vasps)
Title: GwG obligations for crypto asset service providers (VASPs) — §2(1) Nr. 10 GwG
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Crypto exchanges, wallet service providers, and other VASPs registered with BaFin
Text: Crypto asset service providers (VASPs) are obligated entities under GwG and must be registered with or licensed by BaFin before commencing operations. The full GwG obligation framework applies including risk analysis, CDD, SAR reporting, MLRO appointment, and record keeping. Additional VASP-specific requirements: Travel Rule compliance under FATF Recommendation 16 — when executing crypto asset transfers, VASPs must collect and transmit originator and beneficiary information with the transaction; for transfers ≥€1,000, full originator information (name, account address, transaction reference, physical address or national ID number or date/place of birth) must accompany the transfer to the receiving VASP; transfers to or from unhosted wallets (non-custodial wallets) require enhanced CDD to establish the beneficial owner of the wallet. Customer screening: VASPs must screen customer wallet addresses against known illicit addresses using blockchain analytics tools; suspicious blockchain activity (exposure to mixing services, darknet markets, sanctions evasion) is a SAR trigger. BaFin expects VASPs to: implement blockchain analytics as part of transaction monitoring (this is now standard practice for compliant VASPs); monitor for structuring (splitting transactions to avoid thresholds); apply country-specific restrictions consistent with EU sanctions regulations. MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation), applicable from December 2024, overlaps with GwG requirements — VASPs licensed under MiCA must comply with both MiCA and GwG obligations simultaneously. BaFin's AuA for crypto assets contains detailed guidance on CDD for anonymous or pseudonymous wallet interactions.
Keywords: VASP, crypto, §2(1) Nr. 10, BaFin crypto registration, Travel Rule, FATF, blockchain analytics, unhosted wallet, MiCA, screening, transaction monitoring, crypto AML

gwg §56  (bafin_gwg_penalties)
Title: §56 GwG — administrative fines and BaFin enforcement measures
Obligation: MUST (legal consequence of non-compliance)
Threshold: All obligated entities — BaFin and sector supervisors enforce
Text: Section 56 GwG establishes a comprehensive administrative fines framework for GwG violations. Maximum fine levels: for systematic or serious violations by obligated entities: the higher of €1,000,000 or twice the benefit obtained from the violation, or — for credit institutions and financial services institutions — up to €5,000,000 or 10% of total annual turnover; for other violations: up to €150,000. Specific fines for specific violations: failure to appoint an MLRO where required — up to €150,000; failure to conduct a risk analysis under §5 — up to €100,000; failure to apply CDD measures — up to €1,000,000 for credit institutions; failure to report a suspicious activity — up to €1,000,000; failure to maintain records — up to €200,000. Non-financial measures available to supervisors: prohibition on certain business activities; order to remove management personnel responsible for systematic violations; public disclosure of final sanctions decisions (public naming of the entity and violation — this has severe reputational consequences). BaFin publishes all final AML sanction decisions on its website, typically 5 business days after the decision becomes final, with a retention period of 5 years. Criminal prosecution: money laundering itself (§261 StGB) is a criminal offence; facilitating money laundering through negligent failure to apply GwG obligations can expose individuals (MLRO, management) to criminal investigation in severe cases. BaFin's supervisory focus areas include: quality of risk analysis, MLRO effectiveness, SAR quality and timeliness, and beneficial ownership verification.
Keywords: §56 GwG, AML fines, BaFin enforcement, €5M, 10% turnover, MLRO, public disclosure, administrative sanctions, criminal prosecution, §261 StGB, supervisory measures, GwG penalties
