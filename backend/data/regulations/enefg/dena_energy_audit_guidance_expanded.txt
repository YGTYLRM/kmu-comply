enefg_scope_non_sme
Title: EnEfG scope — non-SME definition and applicability
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Companies that are not small or medium enterprises (non-SME) under EU definition
Text: The Energieeffizienzgesetz (EnEfG) and the underlying EU Energy Efficiency Directive impose obligations on companies that do not qualify as small or medium enterprises (SMEs). Under the EU SME definition, a company is an SME if it has fewer than 250 employees AND either annual revenue not exceeding €50 million OR a balance sheet total not exceeding €43 million. A company that exceeds BOTH the employee threshold (≥250 employees) OR either of the financial thresholds (>€50M revenue OR >€43M balance sheet total, if combined with >250 employees) qualifies as a non-SME and is subject to EnEfG obligations. The non-SME classification must be assessed at the legal entity level and also considering affiliated enterprises under the EU group calculation rules — companies that are part of larger groups may qualify as non-SME even if the individual entity appears small. Public bodies and enterprises in which public authorities have a majority holding may also be in scope. BAFA has published guidance on the SME calculation methodology including how to handle consortium structures and holding companies. Companies near the threshold boundaries should conduct and document a formal SME status assessment annually and retain the documentation for BAFA review.
Keywords: EnEfG scope, non-SME, SME definition, 250 employees, €50M revenue, €43M balance sheet, BAFA, energy efficiency obligations, enterprise definition, affiliated enterprises

enefg_edlg_audit_obligation
Title: EDL-G §8 energy audit obligation — 4-year cycle, accredited auditor requirement
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Non-SME companies not operating a certified ISO 50001 energy management system
Text: Under §8 EDL-G (Energiedienstleistungsgesetz), non-SME companies that do not operate a certified energy management system (ISO 50001) must conduct an energy audit at least every 4 years. The energy audit must be conducted by a qualified, independent energy auditor registered with BAFA. Auditor independence requires that the auditor has no financial or organisational relationship with the audited company that could compromise objectivity. The audit must be conducted in accordance with DIN EN 16247-1 (general requirements) and applicable sector-specific parts of the standard (DIN EN 16247-2 for buildings, -3 for industrial processes, -4 for transport). The audit must cover all energy end uses of the enterprise including buildings, industrial processes, internal transport, and compressed air systems. The audit report must contain: a description of the energy flows and energy baseline; identification of all significant energy uses; analysis of current energy performance; identified energy efficiency measures with technical feasibility assessment, investment costs, energy savings potential, and payback periods; prioritised action recommendations. The audit results must be reported to BAFA via the online portal. Companies that implement an ISO 50001-certified energy management system are exempt from the 4-year audit obligation for the period of certification but must still report to BAFA. First-time non-SME companies (newly reaching the threshold) must conduct their first audit within 20 months.
Keywords: EDL-G §8, energy audit, 4-year cycle, DIN EN 16247-1, BAFA, auditor accreditation, ISO 50001 alternative, audit obligation, energy performance, energy report

enefg_7500_mwh_threshold
Title: EnEfG §8(1) — additional obligations for companies consuming more than 7,500 MWh/year
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Companies consuming more than 7,500 MWh of final energy per year across all facilities in Germany, regardless of size classification
Text: EnEfG §8(1) introduces a separate energy consumption threshold that triggers obligations independently of the SME/non-SME classification. Any company — including SMEs — that consumes more than 7,500 MWh of final energy per year across all of its facilities in Germany is required to implement either a certified energy management system (ISO 50001) or a certified environmental management system (EMAS) that encompasses energy management. This threshold is calculated as the total final energy consumption across all energy carriers (electricity, natural gas, heating oil, district heat, process heat, fuels for internal transport) across all company locations in Germany. The 7,500 MWh threshold corresponds approximately to the consumption of a medium-sized manufacturing facility or a large commercial building. Companies that cross this threshold for the first time have a transition period of 20 months to achieve certification. Energy management system certification must be granted by an accredited certification body and must be renewed (recertification) every 3 years. Companies with ISO 50001 certification are also exempt from the EDL-G §8 energy audit obligation. Companies that already have ISO 50001 certification before the threshold is first exceeded can count existing certifications. Consumption data for the threshold assessment must be based on actual measured consumption with documentation.
Keywords: EnEfG §8(1), 7,500 MWh threshold, ISO 50001, EMAS, energy management system, annual consumption, certification obligation, SME energy obligation, final energy consumption, MWh threshold

din_en_16247_methodology
Title: DIN EN 16247-1 energy audit methodology — phases and minimum content requirements
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All companies conducting energy audits under EDL-G §8
Text: DIN EN 16247-1 defines the general requirements and methodology for energy audits. The standard requires a structured audit process consisting of four phases. Phase 1 — Preparatory meeting: audit scope definition; data collection list agreed; access arrangements confirmed; responsibilities assigned. Phase 2 — Data collection: collection of energy bills (minimum 3 years), production/occupancy data, equipment inventories, maintenance records, previous energy audits and measures; on-site visits to all significant energy use sites. Phase 3 — Field work (site visit): walkthrough survey of all facilities; measurement of key energy flows where not available from bills; identification of significant energy uses (SEUs) — the 10 largest energy consumers that together account for at least 80% of total consumption; assessment of current control and monitoring measures; interviews with technical staff. Phase 4 — Audit report: energy baseline by carrier and site; energy performance indicators; description of each significant energy use; matrix of identified efficiency measures covering: technical description, estimated energy savings (kWh/year), investment cost (€), simple payback period, CO2 reduction (tCO2/year), implementation complexity; prioritised recommendation list. The audit report must be signed by the auditor and submitted to BAFA. Audit reports must be retained for 10 years by the company. BAFA conducts spot checks of audit reports for quality assurance.
Keywords: DIN EN 16247, energy audit methodology, preparatory meeting, data collection, site survey, significant energy uses, audit report, energy savings measures, payback period, BAFA submission

iso_50001_energy_management
Title: ISO 50001 energy management system — structure, certification, and benefits
Obligation: MUST (if chosen as alternative to energy audit under EDL-G §8 or EnEfG §8(1))
Threshold: Companies choosing ISO 50001 as the preferred compliance path
Text: ISO 50001 is an internationally recognised standard for energy management systems (EnMS) that provides a framework for organisations to improve energy performance continuously. The standard follows the Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle. Key elements: Energy Policy: senior management must establish and communicate an energy policy committing to continual improvement and compliance with applicable energy requirements. Energy Planning: identify significant energy uses; establish an energy baseline; define energy performance indicators (EnPIs); set energy objectives, targets, and action plans. Implementation: implement action plans; ensure energy management is integrated into procurement (buy energy-efficient equipment) and design (consider energy in new facilities). Checking: monitor, measure, and analyse energy performance against baseline; conduct internal audits at minimum annually; identify deviations and take corrective action. Management review: senior management must review the EnMS at least annually; review inputs include: audit results, energy performance trend, regulatory compliance status, recommendations for improvement. Certification: ISO 50001 certification is granted by an accredited certification body (DAkkS-accredited in Germany); initial certification involves a stage 1 (documentation review) and stage 2 (site audit); surveillance audits annually; recertification every 3 years. Benefits of ISO 50001 over energy audit: ongoing continuous improvement vs point-in-time assessment; exemption from 4-year EDL-G audit cycle; often leads to greater actual energy savings through systematic management; increasingly required in supply chain sustainability requirements of large customers.
Keywords: ISO 50001, energy management system, certification, DAkkS, Plan-Do-Check-Act, energy baseline, energy performance indicators, significant energy uses, management review, continuous improvement, EnMS

enefg_waste_heat_utilisation
Title: EnEfG waste heat obligation — identification and feasibility assessment
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Non-SME companies and companies consuming more than 2,500 MWh/year of final energy
Text: EnEfG introduces an obligation to identify unavoidable waste heat and assess the feasibility of its utilisation. Waste heat (Abwärme) is defined as heat arising from industrial, commercial, or service processes that cannot be avoided through process optimisation and is discharged to the environment without being used. Companies in scope must: inventory all significant waste heat sources within their operations specifying temperature level, quantity (MWh/year), and temporal profile (continuous, seasonal, intermittent); assess the technical and economic feasibility of waste heat utilisation options including: direct reuse within the production process, internal building heating or cooling, feeding into a local or district heating network; document the assessment including cost-benefit analysis for feasible options; report results to BAFA. Where waste heat utilisation is technically and economically feasible, companies are encouraged (but not currently mandated) to implement utilisation measures; BAFA may issue orders where potential is significant and utilisation would be proportionate. Local heating network operators have an obligation to connect and accept waste heat from industrial sources where technically feasible under EnEfG provisions. Companies with waste heat potential are encouraged to contact local district heating operators proactively. The waste heat assessment must be integrated into the energy audit or energy management system where those apply. Temperature levels ≥60°C are most valuable for heat network feed-in; lower temperature waste heat may be suitable for heat pumps.
Keywords: waste heat, Abwärme, district heating, EnEfG waste heat obligation, feasibility assessment, heat network, waste heat utilisation, BAFA reporting, heat recovery, energy efficiency

enefg_bafa_reporting_obligations
Title: BAFA reporting — submission of audit results, EnMS certificates, and consumption data
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All companies subject to EDL-G §8 or EnEfG §8(1)
Text: BAFA (Bundesamt für Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle) is the competent authority for enforcement of energy audit and energy management system obligations. Reporting obligations: companies must submit energy audit results to BAFA via the online portal (www.bafa.de/energie) within 2 months of audit completion; companies with ISO 50001 or EMAS certification must submit certification documents to BAFA within 6 weeks of first certification and within 6 weeks of each recertification; companies must notify BAFA if they newly enter scope (exceed non-SME threshold or 7,500 MWh threshold) within 2 months. BAFA portal requires: company identification data, energy consumption data by carrier for the previous 3 years, audit report or certification documents, auditor identification and accreditation number. BAFA conducts quality checks of submitted audit reports and may request supplementary information within 3 months of submission. Enforcement: BAFA may conduct inspections and request additional documentation; non-submission of audit results is an administrative offence subject to fines up to €50,000 per violation; submission of false information is also a sanctionable offence; BAFA can order audits at company expense if obligations are not fulfilled voluntarily. BAFA publishes statistics on audit results and energy savings potentials. Companies should retain copies of all submissions and BAFA correspondence for 10 years.
Keywords: BAFA reporting, energy audit submission, ISO 50001 certificate submission, BAFA portal, enforcement, fines, administrative offence, energy consumption data, quality check, BAFA inspection

enefg_building_energy_requirements
Title: Building energy efficiency requirements — interaction with GEG (Gebäudeenergiegesetz)
Obligation: MUST / CONDITIONAL
Threshold: Companies owning or operating buildings in Germany
Text: The EnEfG interacts with the Gebäudeenergiegesetz (GEG) to impose building energy efficiency obligations on companies. Under the GEG 2024 amendments: new heating systems installed in existing buildings must use at least 65% renewable energy from January 2024 (for new buildings) and following municipal heating planning deadlines (for existing buildings, varies by municipality, from 2026 onward); oil and gas boilers cannot be newly installed without meeting the 65% renewable requirement; heat pumps, district heating connections, and biomass boilers meeting the 65% criterion are compliant alternatives. For companies in scope of the energy audit or ISO 50001: buildings must be included in the scope of the energy audit or EnMS; building energy audit should assess insulation standard (energy label), heating system efficiency, window U-values, and lighting efficiency; identified building measures should be included in the prioritised measures list. Building energy performance certificates (Energieausweis) must be displayed in commercial buildings >500m² frequently visited by the public. Highly inefficient buildings (energy class F or G for commercial buildings in Germany) may be subject to renovation requirements under future GEG revisions. Companies with large commercial real estate portfolios should track their building energy labels and develop a renovation roadmap. Interaction with CSRD: building energy performance may need to be disclosed under ESRS E1 (climate) for companies in CSRD scope.
Keywords: GEG, Gebäudeenergiegesetz, building energy, 65% renewable heating, heat pump, oil boiler ban, building energy label, Energieausweis, renovation obligation, commercial buildings, district heating

enefg_energy_monitoring
Title: Energy monitoring and metering — data collection for management and reporting
Obligation: SHOULD (monitoring systems) / MUST (data collection for audits and BAFA reporting)
Threshold: All companies in scope of EDL-G §8 or EnEfG §8(1)
Text: Effective energy management and audit compliance requires systematic energy data collection. For companies with ISO 50001: metering and monitoring must be sufficient to track energy performance indicators (EnPIs) and identify deviations from baseline; sub-metering of significant energy uses is required; data must be reviewed at defined intervals by the energy management team. For energy audits: BAFA requires 3 years of energy consumption data by carrier (electricity, gas, oil, district heat, etc.) broken down by facility; bills from energy suppliers are acceptable as primary data source; additional sub-metering data for individual processes is expected for large or complex facilities. Smart meters: companies with consumption above 100,000 kWh electricity per year are entitled to and should request smart meters from their grid operator; smart meter data enables more granular energy analysis. For buildings: heat meters in multi-tenant buildings provide individual consumption data; water sub-metering for steam processes. Energy monitoring software: companies in scope are encouraged to implement energy monitoring software (EMS) to automate data collection, trend analysis, and reporting; real-time monitoring enables rapid response to anomalous consumption (equipment faults, process inefficiencies). BAFA guidance recommends energy benchmarking against sector averages using BAFA's published benchmark data for key energy-intensive industries. Data retention: all energy consumption data used for audit or BAFA reporting must be retained for 10 years.
Keywords: energy monitoring, sub-metering, smart meters, energy data, ISO 50001 monitoring, BAFA data requirements, energy performance indicators, benchmarking, energy management software, data retention

enefg_sme_voluntary_measures
Title: SME energy efficiency — voluntary measures, funding programmes, and supply chain pressure
Obligation: CONDITIONAL / SHOULD
Threshold: SME companies below mandatory threshold — voluntary but encouraged
Text: While SMEs below the energy thresholds are not legally required to conduct energy audits or implement energy management systems, BAFA offers funding programmes to incentivise voluntary energy efficiency improvement. Key programmes: BAFA Energy Consulting for SMEs (Bundesförderung für Energieberatung im Mittelstand — EBM): funding of up to 80% of energy consulting costs (maximum €4,800) for SMEs with annual energy costs above €10,000; BAFA Energy Efficiency Networks: funding for SMEs to participate in regional energy efficiency networks with shared benchmarking; KfW Energy Efficiency Programme (EEP): low-interest loans for investment in energy efficiency measures in commercial and industrial buildings and processes; Federal Funding for Efficient Buildings (BEG): grants for building energy renovation measures. Supply chain pressure: large companies in scope of LkSG and CSRD are increasingly requiring suppliers (including SMEs) to report their energy consumption and carbon footprint; SMEs should anticipate this and establish basic energy data collection practices. ISO 50005 provides a simplified energy management guidance specifically for SMEs. Preliminary energy reviews (mini-audits) can be conducted by BAFA-registered energy consultants to identify quick wins before committing to a full audit. Even without mandatory obligations, energy efficiency investments typically have payback periods of 2-5 years and reduce exposure to energy price volatility.
Keywords: SME energy efficiency, BAFA funding, EBM energy consulting, KfW programme, voluntary energy audit, energy efficiency network, supply chain sustainability, ISO 50005, energy cost reduction, green procurement
