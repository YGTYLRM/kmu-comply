bsi_nis2_entity_classification
Title: Essential vs important entity classification — Annex I and II sectors, size thresholds
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All companies in NIS2 Annex I or II sectors meeting medium or large size criteria
Text: NIS2 distinguishes between essential entities (besonders wichtige Einrichtungen) and important entities (wichtige Einrichtungen). Essential entities are: large entities (≥250 employees OR ≥€50M revenue) in Annex I sectors (energy, transport, banking, financial market infrastructure, health, drinking water, waste water, digital infrastructure, ICT managed services to governments, space) plus certain entities regardless of size (trust service providers, TLD registries, DNS providers, public electronic communications networks). Important entities are: medium entities (≥50 employees OR ≥€10M revenue) in Annex I sectors, and medium or large entities in Annex II sectors (postal and courier, waste management, chemicals, food production, manufacturing of certain products including medical devices, computers, machinery, motor vehicles, digital providers, research organisations). Entities must self-assess their classification and register with BSI. Essential entities face stricter supervision (proactive inspections) and higher penalties; important entities face ex-post supervision. German implementation via NIS2UmsuCG extends scope to additional sectors specific to Germany including certain federal and state authority IT operations.
Keywords: essential entity, important entity, Annex I, Annex II, besonders wichtige Einrichtung, wichtige Einrichtung, NIS2 scope, sector classification, size threshold, BSIG, NIS2UmsuCG

bsi_nis2_risk_management_art21
Title: Mandatory cybersecurity risk management measures — Article 21 NIS2 / §30 BSIG
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities
Text: Article 21 NIS2 requires entities to implement appropriate and proportionate technical, operational, and organisational measures to manage cybersecurity risks. The measures must be based on an all-hazards approach and must cover at minimum: (a) policies for risk analysis and information system security; (b) incident handling; (c) business continuity, including backup management and disaster recovery, and crisis management; (d) supply chain security including security-related aspects concerning relationships between entities and their direct suppliers or service providers; (e) security in network and information systems acquisition, development, and maintenance, including vulnerability handling and disclosure; (f) policies and procedures to assess the effectiveness of cybersecurity risk-management measures; (g) basic cyber hygiene practices and cybersecurity training; (h) policies and procedures regarding the use of cryptography and, where appropriate, encryption; (i) human resources security, access control policies, and asset management; (j) use of multi-factor authentication or continuous authentication solutions, secured voice, video and text communications, and secured emergency communication systems where appropriate. The measures must be proportionate to the size and risk exposure of the entity. Management must approve and oversee implementation. Documentation of all measures is required and must be available to BSI on request.
Keywords: risk management, cybersecurity measures, Article 21, §30 BSIG, all-hazards, MFA, cryptography, incident handling, supply chain security, access control, human resources security, proportionate measures

bsi_nis2_significant_incident_definition
Title: Definition of significant cybersecurity incident — reporting triggers
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities — triggers mandatory reporting obligation
Text: A significant incident under NIS2 / §30 BSIG is one that has caused or is capable of causing significant operational disruption of the services provided or financial loss to the entity concerned, or affects other natural or legal persons by causing considerable material or non-material damage. BSI guidance specifies that the following are indicators of significance: disruption that prevents delivery of the essential service for more than 2 hours; data breach affecting personal data of more than 1,000 individuals; estimated financial impact exceeding €500,000 or 1% of annual turnover; incident involving compromise of critical network or information systems supporting essential service delivery; attack on multiple entities suggesting coordinated campaign; incident involving exploitation of a zero-day vulnerability. Entities must maintain an internal assessment process to evaluate incident significance. When in doubt, BSI guidance recommends erring on the side of reporting — a report that turns out to be below the significance threshold has no negative consequences, while failure to report a significant incident is a sanctionable offence. Security incidents at ICT service providers that affect entities must also be reported.
Keywords: significant incident, reporting trigger, operational disruption, data breach, financial loss, BSI assessment, incident classification, Meldepflicht, cybersecurity incident definition

bsi_nis2_three_step_reporting
Title: Incident reporting procedure — three-step timeline (24h / 72h / 1 month)
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities upon becoming aware of a significant incident
Text: NIS2 Article 23 establishes a mandatory three-step incident reporting procedure to BSI (as the designated CSIRT and competent authority for Germany). Step 1 — Early Warning (within 24 hours of becoming aware): submit an early warning via BSI's MELDEPLATTFORM containing: entity identification, date and time of incident, initial description, affected services. Step 2 — Incident Notification (within 72 hours): submit a more detailed notification containing: updated severity assessment, estimated number of affected users, cross-border impact (if applicable), suspected cause (e.g. ransomware, DDoS, insider), initial indicators of compromise (IOCs), and initial mitigation measures taken. Step 3 — Final Report (within one month of incident notification, or within one month of resolution if ongoing): full incident description, confirmed root cause, attack vectors used, affected systems and data, cross-border impacts confirmed, long-term mitigation and remediation measures, and lessons learned. Where an incident is ongoing at the 1-month deadline, an intermediate report is submitted at the deadline and a final report within one month of resolution. BSI may request additional information at any stage. Entities must designate a point of contact for BSI communications and ensure the contact is reachable outside business hours.
Keywords: incident reporting, 24 hours, 72 hours, one month, early warning, incident notification, final report, BSI MELDEPLATTFORM, three-step reporting, CSIRT, Article 23, §32 BSIG

bsi_nis2_management_accountability
Title: Management body accountability and training obligations — Article 20 NIS2
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities — applies to management board / Geschäftsführung
Text: Article 20 NIS2 establishes direct accountability of the management body (Leitungsorgan — board of directors, Geschäftsführer, or equivalent senior management) for cybersecurity risk management. The management body must: approve the cybersecurity risk management measures required under Article 21; oversee implementation of those measures and take responsibility for non-compliance; receive regular training on cybersecurity risks and risk management practices sufficient to identify risks and assess their business impact; ensure cybersecurity is integrated into strategic planning. Under German BSIG implementation, management body members of essential entities can be held personally liable for negligent failure to implement required cybersecurity measures. A management body decision to permanently waive or substantially reduce cybersecurity investments without adequate justification may constitute negligence. Management cannot delegate ultimate cybersecurity accountability to the CISO or IT department — delegation of execution is permitted, accountability is not. BSI provides guidance that management training should cover: threat landscape, regulatory requirements, incident scenarios, and board-level reporting formats. Training must be documented and repeated at least annually.
Keywords: management accountability, Leitungsorgan, Geschäftsführer, board responsibility, Article 20, cybersecurity training, personal liability, senior management, cybersecurity governance, oversight obligation

bsi_nis2_mfa_requirement
Title: Multi-factor authentication — mandatory for remote access and privileged accounts
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities
Text: Article 21(2)(j) NIS2 explicitly requires the use of multi-factor authentication (MFA) or continuous authentication solutions. BSI guidance specifies that MFA is mandatory at minimum for: all remote access to the corporate network (VPN, remote desktop, cloud portals); all access to privileged accounts (local admin, domain admin, root, service accounts with elevated rights); access to critical network and information systems that directly support essential service delivery; administrative interfaces of firewalls, routers, switches, and network security devices. MFA is strongly recommended (and expected by BSI supervisors) for: all user access to corporate email systems; all access to cloud applications processing sensitive data; developer access to production environments. Acceptable MFA factors include: hardware security keys (FIDO2/WebAuthn — highest assurance); authenticator apps generating TOTP codes; push notifications to registered mobile devices; SMS OTP (acceptable but not preferred due to SIM-swap risk). Entities must document MFA coverage and exceptions. Exceptions must have compensating controls documented and approved by management. Shared passwords for system accounts without MFA are not acceptable for systems in scope.
Keywords: MFA, multi-factor authentication, remote access, privileged accounts, FIDO2, TOTP, VPN, cloud access, authentication, Article 21(2)(j), §30 BSIG, access control

bsi_nis2_vulnerability_management
Title: Vulnerability management programme — scanning, patching, and penetration testing requirements
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities
Text: NIS2 Article 21(2)(e) requires entities to implement vulnerability handling and disclosure procedures. BSI guidance specifies the following minimum requirements for a compliant vulnerability management programme. Vulnerability scanning: critical and internet-facing systems must be scanned at minimum monthly; internal systems at minimum quarterly; scan results must be tracked in a vulnerability register. Patch management SLAs: critical vulnerabilities (CVSS score ≥9.0) must be remediated within 7 days or mitigated with documented compensating controls; high vulnerabilities (CVSS 7.0-8.9) within 30 days; medium vulnerabilities (CVSS 4.0-6.9) within 90 days; patch SLA exceptions require documented risk acceptance signed by management. Penetration testing: comprehensive penetration tests of critical systems and services must be conducted at least annually by qualified testers; scope must include network perimeter, web applications, and internal network; results and remediation progress must be documented. Zero-day and actively exploited vulnerabilities require immediate response regardless of CVSS score. Vendor advisories from BSI CERT Bund must be monitored and acted upon. Vulnerability disclosure policy (VDP) must be published for entities operating public-facing digital services to allow external security researchers to report vulnerabilities responsibly.
Keywords: vulnerability management, penetration testing, patch management, CVSS, scanning, CVE, remediation SLA, CERT Bund, BSI advisories, vulnerability disclosure, Article 21(2)(e)

bsi_nis2_supply_chain_security
Title: Supply chain cybersecurity requirements — Article 21(2)(d) and Article 22 NIS2
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities with ICT suppliers and service providers
Text: NIS2 requires entities to address supply chain cybersecurity risks in two ways. First, under Article 21(2)(d), entities must implement policies and procedures addressing security in supply chain relationships, including security requirements for direct suppliers and service providers of ICT products and services. This includes: maintaining an inventory of critical suppliers and service providers; conducting cybersecurity risk assessments of critical ICT suppliers at least annually; including contractual cybersecurity requirements in supplier agreements (incident notification obligations, right-to-audit clauses, minimum security standards, data handling requirements); assessing supplier security posture using questionnaires, certifications (ISO 27001), or third-party audit reports. Second, under Article 22, the European Commission and member states may designate critical ICT products, services, and processes for coordinated risk assessment at EU level — entities using designated products must comply with resulting requirements. BSI particularly focuses on: cloud service providers, managed security service providers (MSSPs), software vendors with access to critical systems, and telecommunications providers. Software bill of materials (SBOM) documentation is recommended for software-intensive entities. Entities remain responsible for cybersecurity impacts of supplier vulnerabilities — supplier incident notifications must be incorporated into entity's incident response plan.
Keywords: supply chain security, ICT suppliers, vendor assessment, contractual requirements, Article 21(2)(d), Article 22, right to audit, MSSP, cloud provider, SBOM, critical suppliers

bsi_nis2_bcp_drp
Title: Business continuity and disaster recovery planning — Article 21(2)(c)
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities
Text: NIS2 Article 21(2)(c) requires entities to implement business continuity management including backup management and disaster recovery, and crisis management. BSI guidance specifies: Business Continuity Plan (BCP) must cover all essential services and be tested at minimum annually through tabletop exercises; Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP) must address all critical IT systems and define Recovery Time Objectives (RTO — maximum tolerable downtime) and Recovery Point Objectives (RPO — maximum data loss tolerance) for each system; testing must include actual restoration from backup at least once per year. Backup requirements: critical data must be backed up at minimum daily; backups must be stored offline or in an isolated environment not reachable from the primary network (immutable backups); backup integrity must be tested quarterly; backup restoration must be documented and timed against RPO/RTO targets. Crisis management: entities must designate a crisis management team with defined roles; crisis communication plan must cover internal escalation, regulatory notification (BSI), and customer/public communication; contact lists must be maintained and current. BSI expects BCP and DRP to be integrated with the incident response plan so that disaster recovery procedures are activated as part of the incident response workflow. For essential entities in critical infrastructure, additional requirements under KRITIS regulations may apply including redundancy and geographic separation of backup systems.
Keywords: business continuity, disaster recovery, BCP, DRP, RTO, RPO, backup management, offline backup, crisis management, Article 21(2)(c), immutable backup, KRITIS

bsi_nis2_cryptography
Title: Cryptography and encryption requirements — Article 21(2)(h)
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities
Text: NIS2 Article 21(2)(h) requires entities to implement policies and procedures for the use of cryptography and, where appropriate, encryption. BSI guidance (BSI TR-02102 series) specifies: Data in transit: TLS 1.2 is the minimum acceptable version; TLS 1.3 is strongly recommended and required for new deployments; weak cipher suites (RC4, DES, 3DES, MD5 HMAC) must be disabled; perfect forward secrecy (PFS) cipher suites required for essential entities. Data at rest: personal data, financial data, and other sensitive information must be encrypted at rest; AES-128 is the minimum (AES-256 recommended); full-disk encryption required for portable devices (laptops, USB drives); database encryption for critical data stores. Key management: cryptographic keys must be protected with access controls equivalent to the data they protect; key rotation must be documented; hardware security modules (HSMs) recommended for essential entities. Password hashing: passwords must be stored using strong hashing algorithms (bcrypt, Argon2, scrypt) — MD5 and SHA-1 are not acceptable for password storage. Email security: SPF, DKIM, and DMARC must be configured for all domains; S/MIME or PGP recommended for sensitive communications. End-to-end encryption required for voice and text communications discussing critical operational matters. Certificate management: TLS certificate expiry monitoring required; certificate lifecycle management must prevent service outages from expired certificates.
Keywords: cryptography, TLS 1.3, encryption at rest, AES-256, key management, HSM, password hashing, email security, SPF DKIM DMARC, BSI TR-02102, certificate management, Article 21(2)(h)

bsi_nis2_access_control
Title: Access control, identity management, and least privilege — Article 21(2)(i)
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities
Text: NIS2 Article 21(2)(i) requires human resources security, access control policies, and asset management. BSI guidance requires: Principle of least privilege must be implemented — users and systems must have only the minimum access rights necessary for their function; privileged access (administrator rights) must be separate from regular user accounts; privileged access must be managed through a Privileged Access Management (PAM) solution for essential entities. Access reviews: all user access rights must be reviewed at minimum every 6 months; access rights of departing employees must be revoked on the day of departure; access rights of role-changers must be adjusted within 5 business days. Asset management: a complete and current inventory of all network-connected assets must be maintained; shadow IT (unsanctioned devices and services) must be identified and managed; cloud asset inventory must cover all subscriptions and services. Account management: shared accounts must be eliminated or strictly controlled with individual attribution; service accounts must have documented owners and purposes; inactive accounts must be disabled after 90 days of inactivity. Joiners-movers-leavers process must be documented and enforced with HR system integration where possible. Zero trust architecture principles are recommended for essential entities — verify explicitly, use least privilege, assume breach. Access control logs must be retained for minimum 12 months and reviewed regularly for anomalies.
Keywords: access control, least privilege, PAM, privileged access management, access review, asset management, user provisioning, zero trust, joiners movers leavers, Article 21(2)(i), identity management

bsi_nis2_logging_monitoring
Title: Security logging and monitoring requirements
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities
Text: BSI guidance requires comprehensive security logging and monitoring as part of the Article 21 risk management measures. Logging requirements: security events must be logged for all critical systems including: authentication successes and failures, privilege escalation events, firewall allow/deny decisions, DNS queries from internal systems, changes to security configurations, access to sensitive data. Log retention: security logs must be retained for minimum 12 months with at least 3 months in immediately accessible storage (hot storage). Log integrity: logs must be protected from tampering — write-once storage or cryptographic chaining of log entries; logs must not be deletable by the accounts that generated them. Security monitoring: essential entities must implement a Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) system or equivalent centralised log analysis capability; monitoring must include detection rules for common attack patterns (brute force, lateral movement, data exfiltration, ransomware indicators); alerts must be reviewed in near-real-time during business hours with documented on-call procedures outside business hours. Network monitoring: network traffic must be monitored for anomalous patterns; network flow data must be retained for minimum 90 days. Intrusion detection systems (IDS/IPS) are required for internet-facing perimeter systems of essential entities. Monitoring outcomes must feed into the incident response process with documented escalation procedures.
Keywords: security logging, SIEM, log retention, monitoring, intrusion detection, IDS IPS, network monitoring, log integrity, security events, incident detection, Article 21

bsi_nis2_security_training
Title: Cybersecurity awareness training — Article 21(2)(g)
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities — for all employees
Text: NIS2 Article 21(2)(g) requires basic cyber hygiene practices and cybersecurity training. BSI guidance specifies: all employees must receive cybersecurity awareness training at minimum once per year; training must be role-appropriate (general awareness for all staff, technical training for IT staff, risk-focused training for management); training must cover at minimum: phishing recognition and reporting, password hygiene and MFA usage, safe handling of portable devices, social engineering awareness, incident reporting procedures, acceptable use of company IT resources, data classification and handling. Phishing simulation exercises must be conducted at least twice per year for essential entities; results must be used to target additional training for highest-risk users. IT and security staff must receive specialist training covering: current threat landscape, security tools and technologies in use, incident response procedures, relevant regulatory requirements. Training must be documented: employee name, date, topic, duration, completion confirmation must be recorded and retained for 3 years. Management training (separate from general awareness) must cover cybersecurity risk governance, regulatory obligations, and board-level reporting. Training effectiveness must be measured (e.g. phishing click rates before and after training) and improvement targets set annually. Onboarding training for new employees must be completed within 30 days of joining.
Keywords: cybersecurity training, awareness training, phishing simulation, Article 21(2)(g), employee training, security hygiene, management training, onboarding, training records, IT staff training

bsi_nis2_registration
Title: Registration obligation with BSI — all entities in scope must register
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All essential and important entities in Germany
Text: Under NIS2UmsuCG (German NIS2 transposition), all essential and important entities must register with BSI. Registration is conducted via BSI's MELDEPLATTFORM (available at meldeplattform.bsi.de). Required registration information: legal name and address of the entity, sector and subsector classification (Annex I or II), contact details for cybersecurity matters including 24/7 reachable contact for BSI incident communications, IP address ranges and domain names of the entity, relevant member states where services are provided. Registration deadline: entities already in scope must register within 3 months of the NIS2UmsuCG coming into force (October 2024); new entities entering scope must register within 3 months of becoming subject to NIS2. Entities must notify BSI of any material changes to registration information within 2 weeks. Failure to register is an administrative offence subject to fines. Registration is separate from incident reporting — entities must maintain the ability to report incidents through the MELDEPLATTFORM at all times. BSI uses registration data to proactively contact entities in scope with threat intelligence, advisories, and supervisory activities. Entities that are already registered under previous KRITIS regulations must update their registration to comply with the expanded NIS2 scope.
Keywords: BSI registration, MELDEPLATTFORM, NIS2UmsuCG, registration obligation, sector classification, contact information, incident reporting portal, KRITIS, NIS2 scope

bsi_nis2_penalties
Title: Penalties and supervisory measures — NIS2UmsuCG / BSIG
Obligation: MUST (legal consequences of non-compliance)
Threshold: All essential and important entities — supervisory authority is BSI
Text: NIS2UmsuCG establishes a graduated penalty regime under BSIG. For essential entities: maximum fine of €10,000,000 or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover of the preceding financial year, whichever is higher. For important entities: maximum fine of €7,000,000 or 1.4% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Specific fines for specific violations: failure to implement Article 21 risk management measures — up to the maximum; failure to report a significant incident within required timeframes — up to €500,000 for first offence, higher for repeat offences; failure to register with BSI — up to €100,000. Non-financial supervisory measures available to BSI: orders to implement specific security measures by a deadline; temporary suspension of certifications; prohibition of natural persons in management positions from exercising management functions for persistent non-compliance by essential entities (most severe measure, requires proportionality assessment); public disclosure of non-compliance decisions (naming and shaming). Proportionality: fines must be proportionate to the severity of the violation, the size of the entity, and any actual harm caused. BSI has published guidance on its enforcement priorities — the first period of NIS2 enforcement focuses on registration compliance and incident reporting; technical measure compliance will be the focus of subsequent supervision cycles.
Keywords: NIS2 penalties, BSIG fines, €10M, 2% turnover, supervisory measures, BSI enforcement, management prohibition, incident reporting fine, registration fine, proportionality, essential entities fines

bsi_nis2_german_transposition_specifics
Title: NIS2UmsuCG — German-specific NIS2 transposition provisions (October 2024)
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Entities in Germany subject to NIS2
Text: The NIS2UmsuCG (NIS2-Umsetzungs- und Cybersicherheitsstärkungsgesetz), enacted in September 2024 and in force October 2024, transposes NIS2 into German law through amendments to BSIG and other laws. Key German-specific provisions beyond the NIS2 directive minimum: BSI expanded as competent authority with broader supervisory powers including proactive inspections of essential entities, binding orders, and direct fines (previously fines were imposed by Bundesnetzagentur for some sectors); KRITIS regulations (for physical critical infrastructure security) and NIS2 cybersecurity requirements are now more closely integrated — operators of critical infrastructure (KRITIS-Betreiber) face both regimes simultaneously; new category of "besonders wichtige Einrichtungen" (essential entities) with stricter requirements including mandatory security measures certification program planned; federal government entities subject to BSI oversight for cybersecurity; BSI Grundschutz methodology updated to align with NIS2 requirements and may be used as a compliance framework; sector regulators (Bundesnetzagentur for energy/telecom, BaFin for finance, Federal Aviation Authority for aviation) retain concurrent oversight in their sectors; entities must cooperate with BSI investigations within 3 business days of receiving a request. German implementation does not use the voluntary option to exclude entities below the NIS2 size thresholds in critical sectors — all Annex I entities regardless of size are in scope if they meet the specific sector criteria.
Keywords: NIS2UmsuCG, German transposition, BSIG, KRITIS, BSI powers, sector regulators, German NIS2, cybersecurity law, October 2024, Grundschutz alignment, critical infrastructure
