Bundesamt fuer Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle (BAFA) — Guidance on LkSG Compliance
Handreichungen und FAQ, published 2023-2024
Fuer Unternehmen, die dem Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz unterliegen

---

lksg §5  (bafa_lksg_risk_analysis_methodology)
Title: Risk analysis methodology under §5 LkSG — own operations, direct and indirect suppliers; annual update; risk weighting
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All companies in scope of LkSG: from 1 January 2024, companies with registered seat or principal place of business in Germany and 1,000 or more employees including those of German subsidiaries
Text: Section 5 LkSG (Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz) requires in-scope companies to conduct and document an appropriate and adequate risk analysis to identify human rights and environmental risks in their own operations and across their supply chains. The BAFA guidance specifies that the risk analysis must cover three levels: (1) Own operations (eigener Geschaeftsbereich) — the company's own facilities, processes, products, and services in Germany and abroad, including subsidiaries over which the company exercises dominant influence under §2(6) LkSG. (2) Direct suppliers (unmittelbare Zulieferer under §2(7)) — business partners from whom the company directly obtains goods or services necessary for its own products and services. The analysis must cover all direct suppliers, with depth proportionate to the assessed risk level. (3) Indirect suppliers (mittelbare Zulieferer under §2(8)) — the duty to analyse indirect suppliers arises only when the company has substantiated knowledge (hinreichend konkrete Kenntnis) of potential violations. Methodology options accepted by BAFA include: questionnaires and self-assessments; desktop research using existing indices (Transparency International CPI, Global Slavery Index); participation in industry initiatives (sector-specific multi-stakeholder platforms); on-site assessments and third-party audits; engagement with local civil society organisations and worker representatives. The analysis must identify industry-specific risk factors and weight risks by supply chain position (tier 1 suppliers closer to the company bear greater scrutiny obligations than deeper tiers). The risk analysis must be updated annually and whenever there is a significant change in the risk situation — including new sourcing regions, new product lines, or new information about conditions in existing supply chains. Results must be documented and used to derive preventive and remediation measures under §§6 and 7 LkSG.
Keywords: LkSG §5, Risikoanalyse, risk analysis, own operations, direct supplier, unmittelbarer Zulieferer, indirect supplier, annual update, BAFA, methodology, supply chain, risk weighting, human rights risk

lksg §2  (bafa_lksg_human_rights_due_diligence)
Title: Protected human rights positions under §2 LkSG — child labour, forced labour, slavery, adequate wages, freedom of association, health and safety
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All LkSG-obligated companies; applies to own operations and direct suppliers; indirect suppliers where substantiated knowledge exists
Text: Section 2 LkSG defines the human rights and environmental positions that companies must protect through their due diligence obligations. The BAFA guidance provides detailed clarification on each protected position. Prohibition of child labour (§2(2) Nr. 1): no person under 15 years (or under 14 where national law allows light work) may work in ways that impair their health, safety, or education. Hazardous child labour applies below age 18. The ILO Conventions Nos. 138 and 182 are reference standards. Prohibition of forced labour (§2(2) Nr. 2): no work or service may be exacted under threat of violence, confinement, or deprivation of rights — including debt bondage, retention of documents, and restrictions on movement. Prohibition of slavery and comparable practices (§2(2) Nr. 3): covers all forms of exploitation amounting to ownership or control over a person. Prohibition of ignoring occupational health and safety obligations (§2(2) Nr. 5): companies must ensure that applicable national and international health and safety standards are met, including provision of safe equipment, protective gear, and adequate rest periods. Right to adequate wages (§2(2) Nr. 8): workers must receive remuneration sufficient to meet basic living needs, consistent with the applicable minimum wage and ILO standards. Freedom of association and collective bargaining (§2(2) Nr. 6 and 7): workers must be free to organise, join trade unions, and bargain collectively without employer interference. Right to equitable land use (§2(2) Nr. 9): companies must not engage in or benefit from displacement of communities from their land without free, prior, and informed consent. Environmental harms that threaten human rights (§2(2) Nr. 10): production of mercury-containing products (Minamata Convention), use of persistent organic pollutants (Stockholm Convention), and hazardous waste disposal causing human rights harm (Basel Convention). BAFA guidance emphasises that all protected positions must be addressed in the policy statement and risk analysis — selective coverage is not compliant.
Keywords: LkSG §2, protected positions, child labour, Kinderarbeit, forced labour, Zwangsarbeit, adequate wages, freedom of association, health and safety, land rights, environmental harm, human rights, BAFA

lksg §6(4)  (bafa_lksg_direct_supplier_assessment)
Title: Direct supplier assessment under §6(4) LkSG — questionnaires, on-site audits, third-party audits; frequency based on risk; documentation
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All LkSG-obligated companies for all direct suppliers; depth of assessment proportionate to risk score and supply chain position
Text: Section 6(4) LkSG requires companies to implement appropriate preventive measures at direct suppliers, which include conducting assessments of supplier compliance with the LkSG protected positions. The BAFA guidance provides detailed direction on what constitutes an adequate supplier assessment programme. Questionnaires and supplier self-assessments are an accepted starting point but the BAFA has made clear they are not sufficient as a standalone measure for high-risk suppliers or suppliers in high-risk countries or sectors. Questionnaire responses must be verified through additional means. On-site audits are required for suppliers assessed as high-risk based on the §5 risk analysis. On-site audits should assess working conditions, wages, freedom of association, environmental compliance, and the supplier's own management systems. The company may conduct audits directly or engage accredited third-party auditors (certification bodies, specialist audit firms). Third-party audits against recognised standards (SA8000, amfori BSCI, Rainforest Alliance, etc.) may be accepted as partial evidence of compliance, but the company must assess whether the audit scope matches the LkSG protected positions and whether the audit was conducted at sufficient depth. Audit frequency should be risk-based: high-risk suppliers may require annual or biennial audits; lower-risk suppliers may be audited less frequently. Every assessment — questionnaire, audit, or review of third-party certification — must be documented. Documentation must include the date, methodology, findings, and follow-up actions. The BAFA expects companies to be able to provide this documentation during an inspection. Training of procurement staff on LkSG requirements and due diligence methodology is a recommended preventive measure under §6(3) LkSG.
Keywords: LkSG §6(4), direct supplier, Zulieferer, supplier assessment, questionnaire, on-site audit, third-party audit, SA8000, amfori BSCI, risk-based, documentation, BAFA, preventive measures

lksg §9  (bafa_lksg_indirect_supplier_requirements)
Title: Indirect supplier obligations under §9 LkSG — substantiated knowledge trigger; whistleblower reports; investigation obligation
Obligation: CONDITIONAL
Threshold: LkSG-obligated companies; duty arises when the company has substantiated knowledge (hinreichend konkrete Kenntnis) of a potential violation at an indirect supplier
Text: Section 9 LkSG addresses obligations towards indirect suppliers (mittelbare Zulieferer) — those from whom the company does not directly purchase but who form part of deeper supply chain tiers. Unlike the general obligations for direct suppliers, the obligation to act with respect to indirect suppliers is not triggered automatically but arises when the company has substantiated knowledge of a potential violation of a protected position at an indirect supplier. The BAFA guidance defines substantiated knowledge broadly: it includes formal complaints received through the §8 complaints mechanism; credible reports from civil society organisations, trade unions, or media; information from government or regulatory sources; information received through sector-specific multi-stakeholder initiatives; and industry-wide risk alerts. The standard is that the information must be sufficiently concrete to indicate a potential violation — general country-level risk information without specific supply chain linkage is not, by itself, substantiated knowledge. Once substantiated knowledge exists, the company must: (1) conduct a risk analysis specific to the indirect supplier situation; (2) implement appropriate preventive measures; (3) if a violation is confirmed or imminent, implement remediation measures under §7 LkSG. The company must also update its direct supplier contracts to include contractual obligations for the direct supplier to pass down due diligence requirements. The BAFA acknowledges the practical difficulty of tracing indirect supply chains and accepts proportionate approaches — supplier mapping to tier 2/3 for high-risk commodities; participation in sector initiatives that address common indirect supplier risks; engagement with direct suppliers to promote due diligence down the chain.
Keywords: LkSG §9, indirect supplier, mittelbare Zulieferer, substantiated knowledge, hinreichend konkrete Kenntnis, whistleblower reports, investigation, supply chain mapping, BAFA, civil society, complaints mechanism

lksg §6(2)  (bafa_lksg_policy_statement)
Title: Policy statement (Grundsatzerklarung) under §6(2) LkSG — mandatory content; annual publication; senior management approval
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All LkSG-obligated companies; must be published on company website and updated at least annually
Text: Section 6(2) LkSG requires companies to adopt and publish a policy statement (Grundsatzerklarung) on their human rights strategy. The BAFA guidance specifies that the policy statement is a foundational document that signals the company's commitment to human rights and environmental due diligence at the highest level of the organisation. The policy statement must contain: (1) a description of the company's approach to fulfilling its LkSG obligations, including the due diligence procedures the company has established; (2) the human rights and environment-related risks identified as most relevant for the company's supply chain; (3) the measures taken on the basis of the risk analysis; (4) a description of the expectations the company has of its employees and suppliers in relation to human rights and environmental protection. The policy statement must be approved by the senior management of the company — in the case of a GmbH, the Geschaeftsfuehrer; in the case of an AG, the Vorstand. Approval at a lower management level is not compliant. The policy statement must be published on the company's website in a manner accessible to the public — publication only in an annual report or in a supplier portal to which only registered suppliers have access does not satisfy the requirement. The policy statement must be reviewed and updated at least annually. It must also be updated after each risk analysis cycle where material changes in the risk profile are identified. The BAFA notes that the policy statement must address all protected positions under §2 LkSG — a statement that addresses only some human rights issues (e.g. only child labour and forced labour but not environmental harm-linked rights violations) is incomplete. The policy statement must be consistent with the company's actual practices and the outcomes of its risk analysis.
Keywords: LkSG §6(2), Grundsatzerklarung, policy statement, human rights strategy, senior management approval, annual publication, website, mandatory content, BAFA

lksg §8  (bafa_lksg_complaints_mechanism)
Title: Complaints mechanism under §8 LkSG — accessible inside and outside Germany; anonymous reporting; 3-month response; confidentiality; independence
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All LkSG-obligated companies; mechanism must be operational and publicly known before LkSG obligations apply
Text: Section 8 LkSG requires companies to establish and maintain a complaints mechanism (Beschwerdeverfahren) that allows persons inside and outside Germany to report actual or potential violations of the protected positions covered by §2 LkSG. The BAFA guidance specifies the following requirements. Accessibility: the mechanism must be accessible to all persons who may be affected by the company's supply chain operations — including workers at overseas suppliers, local communities, and civil society organisations. Access must not require registration or other barriers that could deter reporting. The mechanism must be available in the relevant languages of the supply chain countries where the company has significant sourcing. Anonymous reporting: the complaints mechanism must allow for anonymous submissions. The BAFA acknowledges that this creates challenges for follow-up communication but requires that at minimum the system accept anonymous reports even where follow-up cannot be guaranteed. Technical solutions (anonymous web forms, anonymous postal addresses) are acceptable. Response obligation: the company must confirm receipt of a complaint and provide feedback on the measures taken within 3 months of receiving the complaint. This response must be communicated to the reporting person if they have provided contact details. Confidentiality: the identity of the reporting person must be protected throughout the process; disclosure is only permissible where required by law. Reprisals against persons who submit complaints in good faith are prohibited. Independence: the mechanism must be operated in a manner that is independent of the operational management who may be the subject of a complaint. The responsible persons or unit must have direct access to senior management. External service providers may operate the mechanism on behalf of the company. The mechanism must be documented and its availability communicated to suppliers and other stakeholders as part of the §6(4) preventive measures.
Keywords: LkSG §8, complaints mechanism, Beschwerdeverfahren, anonymous reporting, 3-month response, confidentiality, accessibility, independence, reprisal prohibition, BAFA, whistleblower

lksg §6  (bafa_lksg_preventive_measures)
Title: Preventive measures under §6 LkSG — contractual assurances; training; supplier selection; supplier development
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All LkSG-obligated companies; preventive measures required for own operations and all direct supplier relationships
Text: Section 6 LkSG requires companies to implement appropriate and effective preventive measures based on the results of the §5 risk analysis. The BAFA guidance details the range of preventive measures and their expected depth. In own operations, preventive measures must include: internal policies and codes of conduct that operationalise the §6(2) policy statement; training of employees — particularly those in procurement, compliance, and operations — on LkSG obligations and the company's human rights expectations; risk-based due diligence procedures integrated into procurement processes. For direct supplier relationships: (1) Contractual assurances (vertragliche Zusicherungen) under §6(4) Nr. 1: new and renewing supplier contracts must include contractual commitments by the supplier to comply with the LkSG protected positions and to extend equivalent obligations to their own suppliers. The BAFA has clarified that including LkSG terms in purchase orders or general terms and conditions is acceptable but suppliers must be genuinely aware of these terms. (2) Training and capacity building: where a supplier lacks the capacity to meet LkSG requirements, the company should provide or facilitate training or technical assistance rather than immediately terminating the relationship, particularly where termination would have severe consequences for the workers involved. (3) Supplier selection and evaluation: LkSG compliance criteria must be integrated into supplier qualification and selection processes. Procurement decisions must give weight to human rights and environmental performance alongside price and quality considerations. (4) Supplier audits: as described in §6(4) Nr. 2, risk-based audits of direct suppliers form part of preventive measures. The BAFA expects companies to have a documented supplier management process that incorporates LkSG due diligence as a standard element, not a one-time exercise.
Keywords: LkSG §6, preventive measures, Praventionsmassnahmen, contractual assurances, training, supplier selection, supplier development, own operations, direct supplier, BAFA

lksg §7  (bafa_lksg_remediation)
Title: Remediation obligations under §7 LkSG — immediate action; phased elimination; contract termination as last resort; documentation
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All LkSG-obligated companies upon discovery or reliable indication of a violation of a protected position in own operations or at a direct supplier
Text: Section 7 LkSG sets out the remediation obligations triggered when the company has discovered a violation of a protected position or when there is a reliable indication (hinreichende Anhaltspunkte) that such a violation has occurred or is imminent. The BAFA guidance distinguishes between remediation in own operations and at suppliers. In own operations: the company must immediately cease the violating practice and take all necessary measures to end and remedy the violation. Remediation must address the harm caused — where possible, including compensation or other forms of remedy for affected workers or communities. At direct suppliers: the company must first attempt to achieve remediation through concerted efforts with the supplier — developing and implementing a corrective action plan with clear timelines, milestones, and monitoring. Immediate termination of a supplier relationship is not the first-resort response; the BAFA guidance emphasises that abrupt termination can harm the very workers the LkSG aims to protect by depriving them of their livelihood. However, where violations are severe and cannot be remediated within a reasonable timeframe, or where the supplier is unwilling to cooperate with remediation, the company must consider suspending or terminating the business relationship as a last resort under §7(2) Nr. 3 LkSG. The decision to terminate must be documented with a rationale explaining why less severe measures were insufficient. All remediation steps — corrective action plans, timelines, monitoring outcomes, and final resolution — must be documented in detail. This documentation supports the annual report under §10 LkSG and must be made available to BAFA on request. The company must also consider how to prevent recurrence of the violation by reviewing and updating the preventive measures under §6.
Keywords: LkSG §7, remediation, Abhilfemassnahmen, corrective action plan, contract termination, last resort, documentation, own operations, direct supplier, BAFA, violation, corrective action

lksg §10  (bafa_lksg_reporting)
Title: Annual report under §10 LkSG — mandatory content; public availability; 4-month deadline after financial year end; BAFA portal
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All LkSG-obligated companies; report must be published within 4 months after the end of the financial year covered
Text: Section 10 LkSG requires companies to prepare an annual report (Bericht) documenting the implementation of their LkSG due diligence obligations during the financial year. The BAFA has issued detailed guidance on the mandatory content and format of this report. Mandatory content: (1) A description of the company's due diligence processes, including the organisation of its human rights due diligence, how responsibility is assigned within the company, and the overall approach taken. (2) The results of the risk analysis under §5 LkSG — including the human rights and environmental risks identified, the methodology used, and the prioritisation of risks. (3) The preventive measures taken in own operations and at direct suppliers under §6 LkSG, with a description of their implementation and effectiveness. (4) A description of any violations identified during the year and the remediation measures implemented under §7 LkSG. (5) The operation and outcomes of the complaints mechanism under §8 LkSG. (6) A forward-looking section describing planned measures for the following year. Publication deadline: the annual report must be submitted to the BAFA portal and published on the company's website within 4 months after the end of the financial year (Geschaeftsjahr). For companies with a calendar financial year, the deadline is 30 April each year. The BAFA has established a dedicated electronic reporting portal through which the report must be submitted. The report must also be made publicly available on the company's website — a report accessible only via the BAFA portal or only in a corporate intranet is not compliant. The BAFA guidance notes that the annual report is also relevant for the CSRD/ESRS reporting obligations where applicable, as supply chain due diligence is a material topic under ESRS S2.
Keywords: LkSG §10, annual report, Bericht, 4-month deadline, BAFA portal, mandatory content, public availability, risk analysis results, preventive measures, remediation, complaints mechanism, website

lksg §13  (bafa_lksg_enforcement)
Title: BAFA enforcement — inspections; orders; fines up to €8M or 2% annual revenue; public procurement exclusion
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: BAFA as competent enforcement authority for LkSG; enforcement actions apply to all in-scope companies
Text: The BAFA (Bundesamt fuer Wirtschaft und Ausfuhrkontrolle) is the competent federal authority for supervising and enforcing LkSG compliance. The BAFA's enforcement approach and powers are specified in §§13–24 LkSG. Inspections: BAFA may conduct inspections (Betriebspruefungen) on its own initiative or in response to complaints from affected persons, civil society, or other stakeholders. During inspections, BAFA may require the company to provide documents, information, and access to premises. Refusal to cooperate is itself an administrative offence. Orders: BAFA may issue administrative orders requiring companies to implement specific due diligence measures, correct deficient risk analyses, improve preventive measures, or cease unlawful practices. Orders may be accompanied by default penalties (Zwangsgeld) to enforce compliance. Administrative fines: LkSG §24 provides a tiered penalty regime. For violations of the core due diligence obligations (failure to conduct a risk analysis, failure to implement preventive measures, failure to establish a complaints mechanism, failure to publish an annual report), fines of up to €800,000 may be imposed for smaller companies. For companies with annual turnover exceeding €400 million, fines may reach up to 2% of average global annual turnover — which for large multinationals can far exceed the nominal cap. The maximum fine is €8 million. Public procurement exclusion: §22 LkSG provides that companies sanctioned by BAFA for serious LkSG violations may be excluded from public procurement contracts for up to three years. This is a significant commercial consequence for companies dependent on public sector business. The BAFA has indicated that it will prioritise complaints-driven enforcement in the early years of implementation, while also conducting sector-specific audits of high-risk sectors.
Keywords: BAFA, LkSG enforcement, Betriebspruefung, inspection, administrative fine, Bussgelg, 2% turnover, €8 million, public procurement exclusion, §22 LkSG, §24 LkSG, orders

lksg §2(3)  (bafa_lksg_environmental_due_diligence)
Title: Environmental due diligence under LkSG — covered environmental violations; Basel Convention; Minamata Convention; nexus to human rights harm
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All LkSG-obligated companies; environmental violations are covered where they cause or threaten to cause human rights harm as defined in §2 LkSG
Text: The LkSG covers environmental violations as a subset of the broader human rights due diligence obligation — but only where the environmental harm is linked to a violation of a protected human rights position. The BAFA guidance clarifies which environmental violations are within the LkSG scope and how the nexus to human rights harm operates. Under §2(3) LkSG, the following environmentally-related prohibitions are covered: (1) Production and use of mercury and mercury compounds in violation of the Minamata Convention on Mercury — particularly the manufacture of mercury-containing products prohibited under the Convention; (2) Production and use of persistent organic pollutants (POPs) in violation of the Stockholm Convention — including certain pesticides (DDT, aldrin) and industrial chemicals (PCBs, dioxins); (3) Export and import of hazardous waste in violation of the Basel Convention on the Control of Transboundary Movements of Hazardous Wastes — particularly the export of hazardous electronic waste to non-OECD countries for informal recycling under conditions that expose workers and communities to toxic substances. The nexus requirement: environmental harm is only within LkSG scope where it causes, or threatens to cause, a violation of a human rights position — such as harm to the health of workers or local communities, or violation of land rights. General environmental harm without a human rights dimension (e.g. CO2 emissions, biodiversity loss unconnected to a community's land rights) is not covered under LkSG, though it may be addressed under the forthcoming Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CS3D). Companies must include an assessment of these environmental risks in their §5 LkSG risk analysis and address them in the policy statement.
Keywords: LkSG environmental, Umweltpflichten, Basel Convention, Minamata Convention, Stockholm Convention, POPs, mercury, hazardous waste, human rights nexus, §2(3) LkSG, BAFA

lksg §3(2)  (bafa_lksg_sme_guidance)
Title: BAFA guidance for smaller in-scope companies (1,000–3,000 employees) — proportionality principle; industry association tools; third-party assurance
Obligation: CONDITIONAL
Threshold: LkSG-obligated companies with 1,000 to 3,000 employees; proportionality principle under §3(2) LkSG applies
Text: The proportionality principle in §3(2) LkSG states that the type and scope of due diligence measures must be appropriate to the nature and extent of the company's business activities, the company's ability to influence, and the typically expected severity of violations, as well as the reversibility of the harm and the probability of a violation. The BAFA guidance provides specific direction for smaller in-scope companies (companies with 1,000 to 3,000 employees that came into scope from 1 January 2024). Risk analysis: smaller companies may adopt a more streamlined risk analysis approach, focusing on the most significant risk factors for their specific sector, sourcing regions, and supply chain structure rather than attempting a comprehensive global analysis from the outset. The BAFA accepts a phased approach for new entrants, provided a basic risk analysis is completed in the first compliance year. Industry association tools: the BAFA endorses the use of industry association (Brancheninitiative) tools — sector-specific risk databases, supplier self-assessment platforms, and collective audit programmes — as a cost-effective means of meeting LkSG obligations. Participation in recognised sector initiatives (e.g. Initiative Chemie3 for chemical industry, amfori BSCI for consumer goods) may be credited towards compliance efforts, though it does not replace company-specific due diligence. Third-party assurance: smaller companies may engage external consultants or audit firms to support risk analysis, supplier assessments, and annual reporting. The BAFA notes that use of third-party expertise does not transfer legal responsibility — the company remains responsible for the accuracy and adequacy of its due diligence. KfW and BAFA offer funding programmes for LkSG implementation support for qualifying SMEs.
Keywords: LkSG proportionality, §3(2) LkSG, SME, Mittelstand, 1000 employees, industry association, amfori BSCI, risk analysis, phased approach, third-party assurance, BAFA guidance

bafa_lksg_sector_specific
Title: Sector-specific LkSG guidance — automotive, garment/textile, electronics, agriculture; high-risk countries identified by BAFA
Obligation: CONDITIONAL
Threshold: LkSG-obligated companies in high-risk sectors; BAFA may develop sector-specific guidance and conduct sector-targeted inspections
Text: The BAFA has developed sector-specific guidance acknowledging that human rights and environmental risks vary significantly by industry and that due diligence measures must be tailored accordingly. Automotive supply chains: high-risk areas include cobalt mining for batteries (Democratic Republic of Congo — child labour, unsafe conditions), aluminium and steel production in countries with inadequate labour protections, rubber and leather supply chains. The automotive sector has established industry platforms (Drive Sustainability, Together for Sustainability — TfS) that BAFA recognises as sources of relevant supplier data. Garment and textile: the sector has a long history of documented violations including child labour, forced labour in cotton production (Xinjiang, Uzbekistan), below-minimum wage conditions, excessive working hours, and unsafe factory conditions. The BAFA cites the Rana Plaza collapse as illustrative of the systemic risks. Sector initiatives (amfori BSCI, Better Cotton Initiative, Fair Wear Foundation) are recognised. Electronics and ICT: supply chain risks include mining of conflict minerals (tin, tantalum, tungsten, gold — 3TG) subject to EU Conflict Minerals Regulation; assembly operations in Southeast Asia with excessive overtime and inadequate safety; e-waste handling at end of product life. Agriculture and food: seasonal agricultural labour in Germany and Southern Europe with documented cases of underpayment and poor housing; cocoa and coffee supply chains with endemic child labour; seafood supply chains with forced labour on fishing vessels. High-risk countries: BAFA guidance references countries with elevated LkSG risk based on Transparency International CPI, U.S. Department of Labor reports, and ILO findings. Companies sourcing from high-risk countries must give priority attention to those supply chains in their risk analysis.
Keywords: LkSG sector, automotive, garment, textile, electronics, agriculture, conflict minerals, cobalt, cotton, child labour, BAFA sector guidance, high-risk countries, supply chain risk

lksg §10(2)  (bafa_lksg_documentation)
Title: Documentation requirements under LkSG — risk analyses, preventive measures, investigations, remediation; 7-year retention; BAFA access
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: All LkSG-obligated companies; documentation obligations arise from §10(2) LkSG and are referenced in §§5–9 for specific due diligence steps
Text: Section 10(2) LkSG requires companies to document the fulfilment of their due diligence obligations and to retain this documentation for a minimum of 7 years. The BAFA has emphasised the importance of documentation as the primary means by which companies can demonstrate compliance during an inspection or enforcement action. The documentation obligation covers all aspects of the LkSG due diligence process: (1) Risk analysis (§5): the methodology used, data sources consulted, risk factors identified, and the weighting and prioritisation of risks. Updates and revisions to the risk analysis must also be documented with their triggering reasons. (2) Preventive measures (§6): copies of supplier contracts with LkSG clauses; records of supplier assessments and audits (including audit reports, questionnaire responses, and corrective action plans); training materials and attendance records; description of integrated procurement processes. (3) Indirect supplier investigations (§9): the information that constituted substantiated knowledge; the risk analysis conducted; the measures implemented and their outcomes. (4) Remediation actions (§7): corrective action plans with supplier sign-off; monitoring records; final resolution documentation; evidence of harm remediation where applicable. (5) Complaints mechanism (§8): all complaints received (maintaining confidentiality of reporters); the review and investigation process; the outcome and response to each complaint. (6) Annual report: copies of each annual report published under §10(1) LkSG. Documentation must be retained for 7 years from creation. It must be stored in a retrievable form — physical or electronic — and be made available to BAFA upon request within the timeframe specified in any BAFA order. Inadequate or missing documentation is treated by BAFA as an indication of non-compliance with the underlying obligations.
Keywords: LkSG documentation, Dokumentationspflicht, 7-year retention, BAFA inspection, risk analysis records, supplier audit documentation, complaints records, §10(2) LkSG, evidence of compliance

lksg §1  (bafa_lksg_2024_update)
Title: LkSG scope extension from 1 January 2024 — companies with 1,000+ employees now in scope; transition guidance for new entrants
Obligation: MUST
Threshold: Companies with registered seat or principal place of business in Germany with 1,000 or more employees (including employees of affiliated companies — §2(6) LkSG subsidiaries); from 1 January 2024
Text: The Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz entered into force on 1 January 2023 with an initial scope limited to companies with 3,000 or more employees. From 1 January 2024, the scope was extended to companies with 1,000 or more employees. This extension significantly expanded the range of companies subject to LkSG obligations — the BAFA estimates that approximately 2,900 additional companies came into scope. Employee count methodology: the threshold counts all employees of the company including those employed by affiliated companies (Konzernunternehmen) over which the company exercises dominant influence under §2(6) LkSG. Part-time employees count as fractions based on contracted hours (a 50% part-time employee counts as 0.5). Temporary agency workers assigned to the company for more than 6 months count toward the threshold under BAFA guidance. Companies at the 1,000-employee threshold that expanded into scope from 2024: BAFA has acknowledged that new entrants had a compressed preparation period. The BAFA's initial enforcement approach towards 2024 entrants emphasises cooperation and capacity building — companies that demonstrate a good-faith effort to implement due diligence processes but have not yet achieved full compliance may receive improvement orders rather than immediate fines. However, complete non-compliance — no risk analysis, no policy statement, no complaints mechanism — will not benefit from regulatory forbearance. Interaction with CSRD: companies that are also subject to CSRD reporting obligations (see ESRS S2 on value chain workers) should align their LkSG processes with their ESRS disclosure framework to avoid duplication of effort. The upcoming EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CS3D/CSDDD) will eventually require comparable measures from a broader group of EU companies and will likely supersede or supplement LkSG requirements.
Keywords: LkSG scope extension, 1000 employees, 2024, new entrants, Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz, BAFA transition, employee count, part-time, temporary workers, CSRD alignment, CS3D
