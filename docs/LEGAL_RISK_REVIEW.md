# Complio — Legal Risk Review

**Scope:** This document assesses Complio's own legal exposure as a SaaS product — not the legal obligations of Complio's customers. It covers: disclaimer adequacy, liability for incorrect AI output, Complio's own GDPR compliance, and commercial law considerations.

**This document is not legal advice.** It is a structured checklist to inform the engagement of qualified legal counsel before commercial launch.

---

## 1. Disclaimer Adequacy

### Current State

Every Complio output carries layered disclaimers:

| Location | Disclaimer text |
|---------|----------------|
| System persona (all LLM calls) | "This output is a preliminary screening, not a legal opinion" |
| PDF report cover | "This report is a preliminary compliance screening, not legal advice. Consult a qualified Rechtsanwalt before acting on these findings." |
| Document templates footer | "Generated by Complio — preliminary template only. Have this reviewed by a qualified legal or compliance professional before use." |
| Product description | "preliminary screening instrument" |

### Assessment

The disclaimers are present and consistently applied. However:

**Risk 1 — Effectiveness under German law:** Under German law (§§ 305–310 BGB for standard terms, plus UWG), a disclaimer may be ineffective if the product's overall presentation creates a reasonable expectation that it provides legal advice. A product that generates "compliance reports" with "legal gap analysis" and "action plans with statutory deadlines" could be argued to create such expectations, regardless of the disclaimer text.

**Risk 2 — Rechtsdienstleistungsgesetz (RDG):** The provision of individual legal services to third parties is regulated in Germany under the RDG. Automated legal compliance screening may fall within the definition of "Rechtsdienstleistung" depending on the level of individualization and the claim of legal accuracy. The RDG contains an exception for automated legal services with low risk (§ 2(3) RDG), but this needs professional legal assessment.

**Recommendation:** Have a Rechtsanwalt review the product presentation and disclaimers and confirm the product does not constitute a regulated Rechtsdienstleistung under the RDG.

## 2. Liability for Incorrect Output

### Risk Assessment

If a customer relies on a Complio report and suffers loss (e.g. receives a regulatory fine because Complio said they were compliant), the question is whether Complio bears liability.

**Limiting factors:**
- Consistent disclaimers stating outputs are not legal advice
- Confidence indicators on findings (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW)
- CANNOT_ASSESS findings when information is insufficient
- `requires_manual_review` flags on partial reports

**Aggravating factors:**
- Product claims to determine regulatory applicability with verbatim law citations — creates an implicit accuracy warranty
- Action plans include "statutory deadlines" — implies legal precision
- CSRD threshold uses pre-Omnibus criteria with a caveat flag — if a customer is incorrectly told they're not subject to CSRD and faces enforcement, liability could be argued

**Standard contract clauses needed:**
- Limitation of liability clause (Haftungsbeschränkung) capping liability to subscription fees paid
- Disclaimer of consequential loss (Folgeschäden)
- Obligation to seek legal advice before relying on screening results
- Exclusion of liability for regulatory changes after report generation

**Recommendation:** Include these in a lawyer-drafted ToS before accepting any paying customer.

## 3. Complio's Own GDPR Compliance

Complio processes personal data of its customers and their employees (who use the platform). This creates GDPR obligations for Complio itself.

### Data Processed

| Data category | Source | Basis |
|--------------|--------|-------|
| Name, email, company | Registration | Contract performance (Art. 6(1)(b)) |
| Billing data | Stripe | Contract + legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(b)(c)) |
| Company compliance profiles | User input | Contract performance (Art. 6(1)(b)) |
| Uploaded company documents | User upload | Contract performance (Art. 6(1)(b)) |
| Usage/session data | Product use | Legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) |

### Current State

| GDPR Requirement | Status |
|-----------------|--------|
| Privacy Policy (Art. 13/14) | NOT YET CREATED — **blocking** |
| Cookie consent (§25 TTDSG) | Frontend has cookie banner check in website scanner but no consent management for Complio's OWN cookies |
| Data Processing Agreement with Anthropic | NOT CONFIRMED — Claude API processes customer data |
| DPA with Supabase | NOT CONFIRMED — Supabase processes customer data |
| DPA with Stripe | LIKELY EXISTS — Stripe is a standard processor |
| DPA with Resend | NOT CONFIRMED |
| Records of Processing Activities (Art. 30) | NOT CREATED |
| Data Retention Policy | Document TTL 7 days implemented; DB retention policy not defined |

### Critical Action Items

1. **Create Privacy Policy** (Art. 13 GDPR + German Telemediengesetz) — required before any data is collected from customers
2. **Review Anthropic's data processing terms** — Claude API sends customer data to Anthropic servers; DPA or contractual safeguards needed
3. **Confirm Supabase DPA** — Supabase has a GDPR DPA available; ensure it's executed
4. **Cookie consent on Complio's own website** — separate from the website scanner feature
5. **Create ROPA** for Complio's own data processing

### Sentry Risk Note

Sentry error tracking is configured with `send_default_pii=False`. However, error traces may contain user IDs, company names, or profile data in stack frames. Ensure Sentry's data processing terms are reviewed.

## 4. Imprint Requirement (§ 5 TMG / DDG)

German law requires any commercial website to display an **Impressum** containing the legal operator's name, address, email, and (if applicable) registration number and VAT ID.

**Status:** Not yet implemented in the frontend.

**Risk:** Operating a commercial website without an Impressum is an administrative offense under German law. Companies can be warned by competitors under UWG unfair competition law (Abmahnung). This is a common enforcement path in Germany.

**Action:** Add an Impressum page at `/impressum` before launch.

## 5. Terms of Service (AGB)

**Status:** Not yet created.

Required content under German law (§§ 305–310 BGB, plus specific e-commerce provisions):
- Identity and contact of operator (§ 312i BGB)
- Subscription terms: duration, cancellation periods, automatic renewal
- Limitation of liability (Haftungsbeschränkung)
- Data processing summary (can reference Privacy Policy)
- Dispute resolution notice (EU ODR platform link required under EU ODR Regulation)
- Jurisdiction and governing law
- Intellectual property: who owns the reports
- Acceptable use: prohibition on using outputs as legal advice

**Recommendation:** Have these drafted by a lawyer specializing in German e-commerce/SaaS terms. Standard UK/US SaaS ToS templates are not adequate for German law requirements.

## 6. Pricing and Distance Contract Law

Under German law (§§ 312b-312k BGB, implementing EU Consumer Rights Directive), consumers have a 14-day right of withdrawal for distance contracts. However, Complio's target market is businesses (B2B), not consumers. B2B contracts do not have mandatory withdrawal rights.

**Action:** Ensure the signup and checkout flow clearly establishes the customer is purchasing for business purposes.

---

## 7. VAT Considerations

SaaS supplied B2B within the EU falls under the reverse charge mechanism. SaaS supplied to German businesses is subject to German VAT (19%) if Complio is a German-based business, or uses the OSS scheme.

**Action:** Consult a Steuerberater on VAT treatment before accepting payments.
